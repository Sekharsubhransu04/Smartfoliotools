How Much Monthly SIP Do You Need to Save ₹1 Crore in 10 Years?

Published: June 3, 2026 Category: Financial Planning Read Time: 6 min

For many Indian investors, accumulating a corpus of **₹1 Crore (₹10 million)** is the ultimate financial milestone. It is a symbol of security, a significant retirement nest egg, or the capital needed to start a business or fund a child's Ivy League education.

But if you set a tight deadline like **10 years**, you are fighting against time. While compounding is incredibly powerful, it works best over 15 to 30 years. Over a shorter 10-year span, the heavy lifting has to be done by your **monthly savings rate**.

In this guide, we will break down the exact monthly Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) required to hit the ₹1 Crore mark in 10 years at different return expectations. We will also look at the massive impact of inflation and show you a smart strategy to reach your goal even if you cannot afford a high monthly investment today.

The Basic Calculations: Hitting ₹1 Crore (No Inflation)

Let's look at the mathematical requirement. Your monthly SIP is invested in mutual funds, compounding monthly. The rate of return is determined by the asset class (e.g., equity, debt, or hybrid).

Here is the monthly SIP required to reach exactly ₹1 Crore in 10 years, assuming different Compound Annual Growth Rates (CAGR):

Expected Annual Return (CAGR) Monthly SIP Needed Total Amount Invested Interest / Returns Earned
10% p.a. (Conservative Hybrid/Large Cap) ₹48,817 ₹58,58,040 ₹41,41,960
12% p.a. (Moderate Equity Benchmark) ₹43,041 ₹51,64,920 ₹48,35,080
15% p.a. (Aggressive Mid & Small Cap) ₹35,887 ₹43,06,440 ₹56,93,560

As you can see, at a moderate return of **12% CAGR** (the historical benchmark for diversified equity mutual funds in India over the long term), you need to invest approximately **₹43,000 every single month** to reach ₹1 Crore.

The Reality Check: Factoring in Inflation

Here is the problem: a rupee today does not buy what a rupee bought ten years ago. Because of inflation (the rising cost of living), the purchasing power of money decreases over time.

If inflation in India averages **6% per year** over the next decade, a corpus of ₹1 Crore in 10 years will feel like just **₹55.8 Lakhs today**.

If your goal is to have a corpus that has the *purchasing power* of ₹1 Crore today, your actual target value in 10 years must be adjusted upward for inflation.

  • Original Target: ₹1,00,00,000 (₹1 Crore)
  • Inflation-Adjusted Target (at 6%): ₹1,79,08,477 (₹1.79 Crore)

To accumulate an inflation-adjusted corpus of **₹1.79 Crore** at a **12% return**, your required monthly SIP leaps from ₹43,041 to a staggering **₹77,080/month**!

This is why ignoring inflation is the number one mistake Indian investors make. You can toggle the inflation adjustment on the Goal Planner Tab to see the difference.

How to Reach the Target with Step-Up SIP (The Cheat Code)

For most middle-class professionals, starting a SIP of ₹43,000 or ₹77,000 per month is impossible. However, you do not have to keep your SIP amount fixed. As your income increases, you can increase your monthly SIP contribution annually.

This is called a **Step-Up SIP**. By committing to increase your investment by just 10% or 15% every year, you can start with a much lower initial SIP.

Let's look at the math for a **12% CAGR** return over **10 years** to reach the unadjusted target of **₹1 Crore**:

  • Fixed SIP (No Step-Up): You start with **₹43,041/month** and pay it for 120 months.
  • 10% Annual Step-Up: You start with **₹27,500/month** in Year 1. In Year 2, you step it up to ₹30,250. In Year 3, it becomes ₹33,275, and so on.
  • 15% Annual Step-Up: You start with **₹21,800/month** in Year 1. It increases by 15% each year.

By leveraging a 10% annual step-up, you can lower your entry barrier by **nearly 36%** (from ₹43,000 down to ₹27,500)! This makes the goal of ₹1 Crore far more achievable. You can project these step-up values on the SIP & Investment Calculator.

Summary of Action Plan

To make your ₹1 Crore dream a reality, follow this checklist:

  1. Determine Your Asset Allocation: To achieve a 12-15% CAGR, you need exposure to equity mutual funds (large-cap, flexi-cap, and index funds). Keep debt mutual funds or FDs for short-term goals, as their 6-7% returns will require a much higher SIP.
  2. Automate Your Step-Up: Set up an auto-escalation instruction with your mutual fund platform so that your SIP auto-increases by 10% every year.
  3. Avoid Redemptions: Do not touch this corpus mid-way for discretionary spending. Let the power of compounding build momentum.
