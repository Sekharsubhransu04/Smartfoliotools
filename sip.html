Frequently Asked Questions

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a method of investing a fixed amount regularly — usually monthly — into mutual funds. Instead of investing a lump sum at once, you invest small amounts consistently. SIP works on the principle of rupee cost averaging: when markets are low, your fixed amount buys more units, and when markets are high, it buys fewer units. Over time, this averages out your purchase cost, reduces timing risk, and helps build wealth through the power of compounding. You can start a SIP with as little as ₹500/month in India.

If you invest ₹10,000 per month via SIP for 15 years at an expected annual return of 12%, your total investment would be ₹18,00,000 (₹18 lakhs). The estimated future value of your SIP would be approximately ₹50.46 lakhs, meaning you would earn roughly ₹32.46 lakhs in returns through the power of compounding. This demonstrates how consistent monthly investments, even modest ones, can grow significantly over long time horizons.

Step-up SIP (also called top-up SIP) is a variant where you increase your SIP amount by a fixed percentage every year. For example, if you start with ₹10,000/month and set a 10% annual step-up, your SIP becomes ₹11,000 in year 2, ₹12,100 in year 3, and so on. This approach accelerates wealth creation significantly because your investments grow in line with your rising income. Step-up SIP is especially powerful for salaried professionals who receive annual increments. Most basic calculators don't offer step-up SIP — Smartfoliotools includes it for free.

Whether SIP or lumpsum is better depends on market conditions and your financial situation. SIP is generally preferred for most investors because it reduces market timing risk through rupee cost averaging — you don't need to worry about investing at the "right time." In a falling or volatile market, SIP performs better. However, in a consistently rising market, lumpsum investment can generate higher returns since your entire capital is deployed from day one. For most salaried individuals in India, SIP is the more practical and disciplined approach to long-term wealth creation.

Historical data shows that equity mutual funds in India have delivered 12–15% CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) over 10+ year periods. Large-cap funds typically return 10–12% CAGR, mid-cap funds 12–15%, and small-cap funds 14–18% over long durations. However, these returns are not guaranteed and past performance does not ensure future results. Debt mutual funds generally offer 6–8% returns. For SIP planning purposes, a 12% expected return is commonly used as a conservative-to-moderate estimate for equity mutual funds in India.
